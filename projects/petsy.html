Petsy: a pet clothing store where a 3D dog or cat tries the coat on before you buy.

Pet clothes get bought on a guess. You can’t hold a jumper up to a cat, and size charts ask for a chest measurement nobody has taken. I designed and built a full store around removing that guess.

TL;DR
The problem

Shoppers size pet clothes from a chart and a photo of a breed their pet isn’t, so the order is a gamble.

The solution

Show the garment on a 3D dog or cat, recommend a size with a reason, and make the rest of the store calm enough to trust.

My role

Everything: reference audit, wireframes, design system, motion, 3D, front-end code, and the Base44 build with its back end.

The UX problemThe shopper has no way to check fit before paying, so the doubt lands on the buy button.
The UI problemMost pet stores are loud grids of discounts. Nothing signals quality or care.
The product problemA return costs the shop more than the jumper earns. Fit is a business problem too.
02 Design principles

Three rules I held every screen to.

01

Show it on the pet

Every page leads toward the fitting room. The 3D model answers what a photo can’t.

02

One decision per screen

Dog or cat, then a category, then a product. No screen asks two things at once.

03

Motion has a job

Animation shows where something came from or went. If it only decorates, it goes.

12 Looking back

What Petsy taught me.

01

Solve the real doubt

Shoppers didn’t need more photos. They needed to see it on their kind of pet.

02

Borrow structure, not style

A proven section order saved time. Type, colour and motion made it Petsy.

03

Wireframe before prompting

The Base44 build worked because every prompt described a section I had already designed.

A challenge project. Petsy is live, but it isn’t a trading shop and has no sales data. The photos come from Unsplash.
