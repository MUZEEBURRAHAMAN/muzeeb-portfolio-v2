CasePro: rebuilding matter creation around what a client says, not what a form demands.

Intake and matter creation used to be one overloaded form. Now they are two connected systems, one AI-assisted and one manual.

Role
Product Designer, system redesign

TL;DR
The problem

Intake and matter creation were one overloaded form, so a paralegal had to know the whole matter before a client had finished explaining it.

What I did

Split them into two linked modules and gave matter creation two paths, AI agent or manual, sharing one six-step structure and one review gate. I brought three options to stakeholders instead of one.

Where it stands

Handed off to engineering with a full spec. It hasn’t shipped, so there is no production data yet, and this case study says so.

01 Overview

CasePro runs a case from the first call to close.

CasePro is the practice-management layer firms use to run a case from the first client call to close: matters, parties, incident facts, documents, deadlines and staffing.

The opening insightI watched intake specialists do the same thing three times in one morning: transcribe a client call into a text box, open a new matter, then retype half of it into six more tabs. The form did the job of two tools and did neither well.

02 The problem

Each new matter started by guessing what the client already told someone.

01

Intake was a text box

The only place client information lived was one freeform field: “Notes or links to intake form.” Nothing structured, nothing reusable.

02

One form, every practice area

The same fields for an auto accident, a slip-and-fall or a commercial dispute. Firms couldn’t tailor questions to their region or practice.

03

Nothing carried forward

Answers from intake never flowed into the matter. Paralegals re-typed names, dates and incident details by hand.

03 Research & diagnosis

The form sat on the wrong data model.

I wanted three independent signals to agree before proposing anything.

ShadowingTwo weeks of live matter creation with intake specialists and paralegals, timing every pause and backtrack.
The CRM’s own dataIn a sample of recent matters, the intake questionnaire field was empty or just a name on most of them.
Reframed interviewsWith the ops lead and two attorneys: “walk me through the last matter you opened,” not “what’s wrong with the form.”

The reframeIntake is what a client tells you, once, in their own words. A matter is the legal record your firm builds from it, and firms need to build it two ways, depending on how much is already captured.

04 Options on the table

I brought three options and rejected the two fastest.

Option A · Rejected

Polish the existing form

Cleaner steps and validation, intake still a field inside matter. A better-looking version of the same bug.

Option B · Rejected

One button, fully automatic

Always generate the matter from intake. Stakeholders liked it first, but many matters start with almost no intake, and a bad guess would land in a legal record unchecked.

Option C · Chosen

Separate intake, let the user choose

Intake becomes its own customizable entity. Matter creation forks: AI-assisted where intake supports it, manual where it doesn’t.

05 Design thesis

Two decisions that reshaped the system.

01

Intake and matter are separate, linked entities

Firms can define their own intake questions per state, county or practice, without touching the matter schema. A voice agent can join a client call and fill structured intake fields.

02

Matter creation needs two paths, not one assumption

Let the person creating the matter say how much is already known, and route them accordingly.

06 Path A · Create with AI

The AI path shows where each value came from.

01

Understand intake

Scores how usable the linked intake is: 17 fields ready, 2 to review, 4 missing.

02

Prepare draft

Narrates each extraction step in the background, visible and cancellable.

03

Review & edit

Every field labels its source: From Intake, AI Matched or AI Suggested.

04

Ask the assistant

Fills gaps where the reviewer is already working.

05

Review before create

The same review gate as the manual path. Nothing skips it.

The rule behind itNothing in a matter should look identical whether a human confirmed it or the model guessed it.

08 Results & impact

Back to the paralegal retyping the same story into six tabs.

Intake specialistsThe notes they capture on the call are the record the matter is built from, shaped to their firm’s questions.
ParalegalsThey confirm an AI draft field by field, or fill one guided form. They type each fact once.
Attorneys & adminsEvery matter arrives as the same reviewable record, and firms reshape intake without an engineering ticket.

What’s designed vs. what’s measured. These outcomes come from design review and the engineering handoff spec, not production adoption. I’d rather say that than invent a metric.

09 What I learned

Three lessons from CasePro.

01

Check the data model before the screen

The form was correct for the data model it sat on. The redesign only worked once the model changed.

02

Confidence needs a visible source

Provenance tags and readiness scores make trust checkable, not assumed.

03

A fallback path erodes over time

Manual creation stays usable only if it’s built and maintained as a first-class path.

Splitting intake from matter added screens. It also let firms shape intake to their own practice.

10 Questions I expect

Questions I expect

Why not ship the one-click AI version stakeholders liked?

The CRM data showed many matters start with incomplete intake. As the only path, it would have broken on the common case and written guesses into legal records.

Has this shipped?

No. It’s at engineering handoff, so there’s no production data yet.
